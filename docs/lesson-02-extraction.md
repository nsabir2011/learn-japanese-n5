# Genki I Lesson 2 extraction: かいもの (Shopping)

Source: *Genki I*, Lesson 2, PDF pages 54–79 (printed textbook pages 56–81).

Purpose: structured source notes for a future guided Lesson 2 HTML experience. This is not the HTML implementation and is not intended as a page-for-page reproduction.

## Lesson outcomes

The lesson teaches learners to:

- ask and answer how much things cost;
- shop and select an item;
- order food in a restaurant;
- identify things by distance from speaker/listener;
- ask where places and objects are;
- ask and answer who owns something;
- say that two things share a property with も;
- negate noun sentences;
- use sentence-final ね and よ;
- understand Japanese prices, larger numbers, and basic currency conventions.

## Dialogue 1: Mary at a flea market

1. メアリー：すみません。これは いくらですか。  
   Excuse me. How much is this?
2. みせのひと：それは さんぜんえんです。  
   That is 3,000 yen.
3. メアリー：たかいですね。じゃあ、あのとけいは いくらですか。  
   It is expensive, isn't it? Then, how much is that watch over there?
4. みせのひと：あれは さんぜんごひゃくえんです。  
   That is 3,500 yen.
5. メアリー：そうですか。あれも たかいですね。  
   I see. That is expensive, too.
6. みせのひと：これは せんはっぴゃくえんですよ。  
   This one is 1,800 yen, I assure you.
7. メアリー：じゃあ、そのとけいを ください。  
   Then, please give me that watch.

Afterward, a stranger finds Mary's wallet:

8. しらないひと：これは だれの さいふですか。  
   Whose wallet is this?
9. メアリー：わたしの さいふです。  
   It is my wallet.
10. メアリー：ありがとうございます。  
    Thank you very much.

Conversation jobs embedded in this dialogue:

- get attention politely with すみません;
- ask a price with X は いくらですか;
- react with たかいですね and acknowledge new information with そうですか;
- contrast これ／それ／あれ and この／その／あの + noun;
- mark “also/too” with も;
- make a concrete purchase request with X をください;
- ask ownership with だれの + noun and answer with person + の + noun.

## Dialogue 2: Mary at a restaurant

1. みせのひと：いらっしゃいませ。メニューを どうぞ。  
   Welcome. Here is the menu.
2. メアリー：どうも。これは なんですか。  
   Thank you. What is this?
3. みせのひと：どれですか。ああ、とんかつです。  
   Which one? Oh, it is tonkatsu.
4. メアリー：とんかつ？さかなですか。  
   Tonkatsu? Is it fish?
5. みせのひと：いいえ、さかなじゃないです。にくです。おいしいですよ。  
   No, it is not fish. It is meat. It is delicious, I assure you.
6. メアリー：じゃあ、これを おねがいします。  
   Then, I will have this, please.

Later:

7. メアリー：すみません。トイレは どこですか。  
   Excuse me. Where is the restroom?
8. みせのひと：あそこですよ。  
   It is over there.
9. メアリー：ありがとうございます。  
   Thank you very much.

Conversation jobs embedded in this dialogue:

- welcome a customer with いらっしゃいませ;
- offer something with X をどうぞ;
- respond casually to thanks with どうも in this context;
- ask what an object is with これはなんですか and clarify with どれですか;
- ask a yes/no identity question with X ですか;
- negate a noun identity with X じゃないです;
- reassure/inform with よ;
- order with X をおねがいします;
- ask location with X はどこですか and answer with ここ／そこ／あそこです.

## Grammar

### 1. これ／それ／あれ／どれ

These pronouns stand alone and refer to things:

- これ: near the speaker (“this one here”)
- それ: near the listener (“that one by you”) or something the listener just mentioned
- あれ: away from both speaker and listener (“that one over there”)
- どれ: which one among alternatives

Core frames:

- これは いくらですか。— How much is this?
- それは さんぜんえんです。— That is 3,000 yen.
- あれは わたしの ペンです。— That over there is my pen.
- どれですか。— Which one is it?

Important constraints:

- The れ-series stands on its own; it is followed directly by particles such as は.
- Question words such as どれ and なに cannot take topic は in the intended beginner question pattern; use が when a particle is required: どれが あなたの ペンですか。
- Avoid using これ／それ／あれ for a person within earshot when introducing them; use the person's name or この／その／あの + person noun. The れ-series can be acceptable for people in an earlier passage or in a picture.

### 2. この／その／あの／どの + noun

These forms cannot stand alone. They must modify a following noun:

- この noun: near the speaker
- その noun: near the listener
- あの noun: far from both
- どの noun: which noun

Examples:

- このとけいは いくらですか。— How much is this watch?
- そのとけいは さんぜんえんです。— That watch in your hand is 3,000 yen.
- あのがくせいは りゅうがくせいです。— That student over there is an international student.
- どのがくせいが にほんじんですか。— Which student is Japanese?

The の-series can refer to people naturally, unlike the standalone れ-series in many face-to-face contexts.

### 3. ここ／そこ／あそこ／どこ

Location words follow the same distance system:

- ここ: here, near me
- そこ: there, near you
- あそこ: over there, away from both of us
- どこ: where

Core exchange:

- すみません。ゆうびんきょくは どこですか。— Excuse me. Where is the post office?
- （ゆうびんきょくは）あそこです。— It is over there.

The lesson only introduces pointing to a location; more specific directions come later.

### 4. だれの + noun (whose?)

Add possessive/linking の to だれ:

- これは だれの かばんですか。— Whose bag is this?
- それは ソラさんの かばんです。— That is Sora's bag.

When the owned noun is obvious, it can be omitted:

- これは だれのですか。— Whose is this?
- わたしのです。— It is mine.

### 5. Noun + も (also/too)

も replaces は on the item that is “also” true:

- たけしさんは にほんじんです。— Takeshi is Japanese.
- ゆいさんも にほんじんです。— Yui is Japanese, too.

Pattern:

- A は X です。
- B も X です。

Scope matters: も goes directly after the parallel item. It does not simply attach to the end of the whole sentence. It also cannot express two simultaneous identities of one person (“Pat is Japanese and also American”) in this beginner pattern; that requires another construction.

### 6. Noun じゃないです (noun negation)

To negate X は Y です when Y is a noun, replace です with a negative form:

- conversational: X は Y じゃないです。
- conservative/formal speech: X は Y じゃありません。
- formal/written: X は Y ではありません。

Example:

- やまださんは がくせいじゃないです。— Mr. Yamada is not a student.

In a negative answer, repeat the noun:

- A：にほんじんですか。
- B：いいえ、にほんじんじゃないです。
- Not: × いいえ、じゃないです。

This pattern applies to nouns, not い-adjectives. Thus たかい and おいしい cannot be negated by simply replacing です with じゃないです; their negative conjugation is taught later.

### 7. Sentence-final ね and よ

- ね seeks confirmation or agreement: “right?”, “isn't it?”
- よ presents information with confidence or emphasis: “I tell you”, “you know”, “let me assure you”

Examples:

- リーさんの せんこうは ぶんがくですね。— Ms. Lee, your major is literature, right?
- これは にくじゃないですね。— This is not meat, is it?
- とんかつは さかなじゃないですよ。— Let me assure you, tonkatsu is not fish.
- スミスさんは イギリスじんですよ。— In case you are wondering, Mr. Smith is British.

## Request and offering expressions

### （～を）ください

Means “Please give me X” and requests a concrete item:

- そのとけいを ください。— Please give me that watch.

### （～を）おねがいします

Also requests item X and sounds slightly more upscale/polite for concrete objects, especially restaurant orders:

- これを おねがいします。— I will have this, please.

It can also request abstract things such as repairs, explanations, or understanding, whereas ください in this lesson's pattern is presented for concrete items.

### （～を）どうぞ

Used while offering or handing item X to someone:

- メニューを どうぞ。— Here is the menu / Please take the menu.

Pronunciation changes to teach explicitly:

- 300 さんびゃく, 600 ろっぴゃく, 800 はっぴゃく
- 3,000 さんぜん, 8,000 はっせん

Japanese groups large numbers by 10,000 (まん), not by 1,000. For example:

- 20,000 = にまん (2 × 10,000), not a “twenty thousand” construction based on せん
- 234,567 = にじゅうさんまん よんせん ごひゃく ろくじゅう なな
- 1,000,000 = ひゃくまん (100 × 10,000)

Price frame:

- X は いくらですか。— How much is X?
- [number] えんです。— It is [number] yen.

Culture note in the source (historical to this edition): the pictured currency includes ¥10,000, ¥5,000, ¥2,000, and ¥1,000 notes, and ¥500, ¥100, ¥50, ¥10, ¥5, and ¥1 coins. Notes and coins have different sizes. The book notes that credit cards and mobile payments are widespread, while some small shops and restaurants may still require cash. The people/designs shown on banknotes are edition-era examples and should not be treated as a timeless list in a future lesson.

## Suggested structure for the future guided HTML lesson

1. **Point and identify** — これ／それ／あれ／どれ with physical perspective.
2. **Name the object** — この／その／あの／どの + noun and object vocabulary.
3. **Shop with numbers** — price questions, irregular readings, まん, reactions, and selecting an item.
4. **Find places** — ここ／そこ／あそこ／どこ.
5. **Ownership** — だれの, person + の, and omitted nouns (わたしのです).
6. **Connect and correct facts** — も and noun negation.

8. **Restaurant mission** — どうぞ, ください, おねがいします, food vocabulary, and a complete ordering exchange.

## Accuracy/design cautions for implementation

- Distance is relational, not a fixed translation: それ is typically near the listener; あれ is far from both.
- Keep これ/それ/あれ (standalone) distinct from この/その/あの (must precede a noun).
- Teach どれ/どの with が in the question patterns shown, not は.
- Do not use the れ-series casually to point at a person who is present.
- も replaces は on the parallel topic; it does not mean that it always belongs at sentence end.
- Noun じゃないです cannot be generalized to い-adjectives such as たかい or おいしい.
- A negative noun answer needs the noun: にほんじんじゃないです, not just じゃないです.
- Preserve the pragmatic contrast: ね seeks shared confirmation; よ supplies/asserts information.
- Treat the banknote portraits/designs as historical edition content; teach denominations and cash conventions without implying that those pictured notes are current forever.
- Practice should include viewpoint changes (speaker/listener swap), because that is essential to mastering こ・そ・あ・ど.
